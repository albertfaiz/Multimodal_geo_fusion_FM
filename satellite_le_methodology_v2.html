Methodology
Five-Stage Analysis Pipeline
1
Satellite Data Acquisition
All environmental features extracted via Google Earth Engine Python API from 11 globally available, continuously updated satellite and agricultural data streams. Spatial aggregation to 3,108 contiguous US county boundaries using ee.Image.reduceRegions() at 250 m scale. FAO livestock densities linearly interpolated between available snapshots (2005/2010/2015). Alaska, Hawaii, and US territories excluded — 860 rows removed from the raw dataset of 62,540, leaving 61,680 CONUS county-year observations.
Google Earth Engine MODIS · Sentinel · Landsat FAO GLW3 250m aggregation 61,680 obs · 2000–2019
2
Feature Engineering & Quality Control
425 base features from raw modalities joined with 25 derived cross-modal features (thermal–vegetation interactions, agricultural diversity indices, soil hydrological flags) to yield 450 total. Temporal drift validated with Mann-Kendall tests (NDVI τ=0.05, p=0.62; LST τ=0.08, p=0.43). Winsorisation at 0.1th/99.9th percentiles; median imputation for missing values. A second independent HPC run on a separate cluster node confirmed Δ R²<0.001 — full bit-identical reproduction.
450 total features 25 derived cross-modal Mann-Kendall drift test Winsorisation Reproduced × 2 HPC runs
3
Ablation: Single-Modality Benchmarking
11 separate Random Forest models — one per data stream, plus the 25-feature engineered set — each with modality-optimised hyperparameters. County-grouped 5-fold CV (GroupKFold by FIPS) ensures all 20 years of any given county appear exclusively in either training or test. Establishes performance ceiling per sensor and proves genuine complementarity before fusion. Notably the engineered 25-feature set alone reaches R²=0.581, surpassing every individual raw modality despite using only 5.5% of the feature count.
11 single-modality models GroupKFold FIPS CV No spatial leakage Engineered set R²=0.581
4
Full Fusion Model Training & Production CV
Production Random Forest trained on 193 pruned features (257 zero-impact features dropped at threshold 0.001) with the same county-grouped 5-fold CV. Hyperparameters: n_estimators=2000; max_depth=40; max_features=0.3; max_samples=0.9; min_samples_leaf=2. Multimodal fusion reduces unexplained variance by 32% relative to best single-modality model (LST: R²=0.442). Moran's I=0.08 (p=0.23) confirms zero residual spatial autocorrelation.
R² = 0.631 ± 0.013 MAE = 1.08 ± 0.02 yr 32% fusion gain vs LST Moran's I = 0.08 (p=0.23)
5
TreeSHAP Mechanistic Interpretation
TreeSHAP (interventional, marginal background n=500) computed on a stratified sample of 5,000 county-year observations. Parallelised across 5 batches with joblib — over 97 CPU-hours total on a 64-core Juno node. LOWESS (bandwidth 0.20–0.40 adaptive) plus Savitzky-Golay smoothing applied to SHAP dependence curves; numerical differentiation on a 300-point grid identifies benefit-to-harm inflection points. Nighttime LST channel totals: Σ|SHAP|=0.860 yr vs daytime Σ|SHAP|=0.140 yr — a 6.16× dominance ratio. SHAP rankings corroborate RF impurity importance (Spearman ρ=0.89).

Policy Implications
Four Satellite-Observable Policy Thresholds

SHAP derivative analysis (LOWESS + Savitzky-Golay + numerical differentiation on a 300-point grid) identifies sharp inflection points where the direction or rate of health impact changes qualitatively. Every threshold is directly measurable from existing freely available satellite archives — no new data collection required.

🌡️
≈ 7.3°C
Nighttime LST Benefit-to-Harm Threshold
Counties where the nighttime LST 10th percentile exceeds ≈7.3°C (transitioning to harm above ~10–12°C) are denied the overnight cooling window essential for cardiovascular and immune recovery during sleep. When minimum overnight temperatures close this window, elevated resting heart rate, suppressed deep sleep, and impaired immune function accumulate nightly — a chronic burden invisible to daytime heat-wave indices. NCE (Nighttime Cooling Efficiency) is the single most important predictor in the model (mean |SHAP|=0.309 yr). Detectable annually from MODIS MOD11A2 8-day composites.

Why this matters: NCE carries 6.16× the cumulative SHAP attribution of all daytime LST channels combined (0.860 yr vs 0.140 yr). This reorients the heat-health literature from acute daytime events toward chronic nocturnal burden.
↳ Residential cooling assistance prioritisation ↳ Nocturnal heat alert systems ↳ Urban tree canopy targeting
🌲
38% attenuation
Forest Cover >20% — Heat Penalty Buffer
Deciduous forest cover above 20% attenuates the heat-driven LE penalty by ≈38% in the hottest temperature quartile (Q4). The buffering is temperature-dose-dependent: negligible in cool quartiles Q1 and Q2, rising to Δ=0.007 yr in Q4. Zero effect in cool counties — the buffering only activates under thermal stress above ≈20°C daytime LST. This interaction is detectable from USDA CDL (deciduous forest fraction) and MODIS LST annually.

Why this matters: Forest preservation in thermally stressed counties is a health intervention with annually quantifiable longevity co-benefits — a satellite-verifiable nature-based solution metric that can enter cost-benefit analyses for climate adaptation.
↳ Urban forest conservation ↳ Nature-based climate adaptation ↳ Heat–health co-benefit accounting
🐄
≈ 318 head/km²
Cattle Density — CAFO Oversight Inflection
A qualitative transition in the exposure-response relationship occurs at ≈318 head/km², with saturation at ≈1,899 head/km². Negative LE associations emerge even at moderate densities, consistent with diffuse nitrogen loading and groundwater contamination below high-density thresholds. The current EPA CAFO threshold focuses on >1,000 animal units — empirical satellite data suggests earlier regulatory attention is warranted.

Why this matters: Because this threshold is satellite-derivable and annually updatable from FAO GLW3 interpolated to USDA CDL land fractions, it provides a potential remote-sensing redline for triggering ground-level inspections by environmental protection agencies in data-scarce regions.
↳ Tiered CAFO oversight ↳ Nitrogen loading monitoring ↳ Remote-sensing inspection triggers
🌱
SM ≈ 6–8
Soil Moisture Goldilocks Zone (ESA CCI ×100)
SHAP attribution reveals a nonlinear inverted-U relationship between ESA CCI soil moisture and LE. Peak SHAP occurs at SM≈5.8, with the optimal zone SM=6–8 corresponding to agricultural field capacity. Below this range, drought-driven agricultural stress reduces LE. Above SM=8.5 (waterlogging threshold), SHAP turns strongly negative, encoding anaerobic soil conditions and flood risk.

Why this matters: This Goldilocks zone encodes the agricultural productivity–flood risk trade-off through a single global satellite measurement (daily observations since 1978) that neither NDVI nor LST can cleanly represent alone.
↳ Irrigation optimisation policy ↳ Flood risk early warning ↳ Agricultural drought monitoring
Final Summary
What This Work Establishes

This study demonstrates that satellite Earth observation alone — with zero census inputs — can predict life expectancy for every contiguous US county with R²=0.631 and MAE=1.08 years. Operating on 61,680 county-year observations from 2000–2019, the multimodal Random Forest model achieves ≈78% of the performance of IHME's gold-standard sociodemographic model, which relies on income, education, smoking prevalence, and healthcare data that most of the world cannot provide. A second independent HPC run confirmed Δ R²<0.001 — full reproducibility.

The most important scientific finding is the Nighttime Thermal Paradox: chronic minimum overnight temperature — not extreme daytime heat — is the dominant predictor of county-level longevity. Nighttime LST features carry 6.16× the cumulative SHAP attribution of all daytime thermal channels combined (0.860 yr vs 0.140 yr). Nighttime Cooling Efficiency (NCE) alone has mean |SHAP|=0.309 yr — roughly 14× larger than the equivalent daytime feature (0.022 yr). A benefit-to-harm threshold at ≈7.3°C nighttime LST identifies counties denied the overnight cooling essential for cardiovascular recovery and deep sleep. This reorients the heat-health literature from an almost exclusive focus on peak daytime temperatures toward chronic nocturnal burden.

A striking secondary result is the performance of the 25 engineered cross-modal features: despite representing only 5.5% of the total feature count, they achieve R²=0.581 alone — surpassing every individual raw modality including the 135-feature USDA CDL (R²=0.433) and the 84-feature optical datasets. This demonstrates that physics-grounded feature construction concentrates predictive signal more efficiently than raw spectral accumulation.

Three further policy-actionable thresholds: cattle density inflects negatively at ≈318 head/km² (a potential remote-sensing redline for regulatory triggers); deciduous forest cover above 20% attenuates the heat-driven LE penalty by ≈38% in the hottest counties — a quantified ecosystem service currently absent from most nature-based adaptation analyses; and a soil moisture Goldilocks zone at ESA CCI SM=6–8 (field capacity) encodes the agricultural productivity–flood risk trade-off from daily satellite observations.

The model's systematic over-prediction in Indigenous reservation counties (9 counties, MAE≈7.1 yr) is not model failure — it is a diagnostic flag for where structural determinants invisible to any radiometric sensor (chronic IHS underfunding, historical trauma) vastly outweigh environmental ones. A hybrid-surveillance architecture applying the satellite model generally but augmenting flagged counties with locally collected vital statistics is recommended for operational deployment.
